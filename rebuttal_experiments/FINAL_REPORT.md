## 4. Methods and configurations

Shared MLP: input `[x‖t]`, 3 hidden×128, Swish, no norm/dropout/residual. Params ≈ 33.9k (d2) … 66.3k (d256). Optimizer Adam lr 1e-3, wd 0, no scheduler/clip/EMA. **Training batch 4096** (paper value; see §6 batch caveat), 10k steps. Sampling default RK4 N=128 (NFE 512), n_gen 10k. Seeds {8925, 77395, 65457}; split_seed 0; matrix_seed 42.

| Method | Source | Path | Norm-preserving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian FM | N(0,I) | Euclidean | no |
| Source-only (oracle/empirical) | analytic / eCDF radial | Euclidean | no |
| RAFM (oracle/empirical) | analytic / eCDF radial | spherical geodesic (slerp) + tangent projection | yes |
| MSGM | multiplicative-noise SDE, eCDF prior | Stratonovich SDE | optional |

**Central mechanism (verified):** with tangent projection, ‖x_t‖=‖x_0‖ exactly, so RAFM's radial law = its source's radial law, independent of the trained net (E4 drift → 6.5e-6). The net learns only angular rotation. Radial metrics therefore test the *source*; angular/global metrics test the *path*.

### 5.8 E8 — Tail-heaviness scaling (addresses R8)
Student-t d16, df ∈ {1.5,2,3,5,10,50} (heavy → near-Gaussian), 3 seeds. Files: `tables/E8_tail_scaling.*`, `figures/E8_tail_scaling.png`. radial_w1 (mean):

| method | df1.5 | df2 | df3 | df5 | df10 | df50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gaussian_fm | 44.3 | 19.1 | 1.82 | 0.470 | 0.191 | 0.082 |
| source_only_empirical | 8.65 | 3.35 | 0.354 | 0.203 | 0.212 | 0.140 |
| rafm_empirical | 4.02 | 1.07 | 0.238 | 0.100 | 0.156 | 0.073 |

**Monotone, and self-limiting.** For heavy tails RAFM's advantage is large (df=1.5: RAFM 4.0 vs Gaussian 44.3 ≈ 11×; vs source-only ≈ 2×). As tails lighten toward Gaussian the gap closes and RAFM becomes neutral: at df=50 Gaussian-FM 0.082 ≈ RAFM 0.073; at df=10 Gaussian-FM (0.191) even slightly beats RAFM-empirical (0.156) on radial. This is the desired behavior — RAFM helps exactly when the source mismatch is real (heavy tails) and neither helps nor meaningfully hurts on light-tailed data. sliced_w1 shows RAFM ≤ baselines at every df with a shrinking margin.

### 5.10 E-Finance — Kenneth French 49 Industry daily returns (new real dataset, addresses R-realworld)
d=49, N=14,349 daily industry-return vectors (1969–2026 after dropping pre-1969 -99.99 sentinels), **chronological** 60/20/20, train-only standardization. Quick check (`dataset_notes/finance_quick_check.md`): marginal excess kurtosis 11.2, radial excess kurtosis 41.3 (very heavy-tailed), covariance condition number 196 (anisotropic, market factor dominant). Files: `tables/E_finance_ff49.*`, `raw_results/E_finance/`, `raw_results/E3_angular/E_finance_ff49.csv`.

| method | radial_w1 | ks_stat | sliced_w1 | angular_sw | q995_err | train_s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gaussian_fm | 1.418±0.11 | 0.218±0.009 | 0.194±0.018 | 0.0236 | 0.311 | 18 |
| source_only_empirical | 1.707±0.04 | 0.246±0.001 | 0.208±0.005 | 0.0205 | 0.350 | 19 |
| msgm (3 seeds) | 1.417 | 0.197 | 0.182 | 0.0200 | — | 19307 |
| rafm_empirical | 1.423±0.04 | 0.200±0.003 | **0.174±0.003** | **0.0173** | 0.381 | 35 |

MSGM (3 seeds, §5.17): on radial it is **statistically tied with RAFM and Gaussian-FM** (1.417±0.033 vs 1.423 vs 1.418 — all pinned at the chronological shift floor 1.386; the earlier 1-seed 1.373 was noise), marginally best on KS; **RAFM remains best on the global/directional metrics (sliced_w1, MMD, angular_sw)** and trains **~550× faster** (35 s vs ~5.4 h). So on finance no method beats the radial shift floor — an honest, non-"wins-everywhere" outcome; RAFM's edge here is directional + speed.

**Neutral-radial / favorable-direction — and an important methodological point.** Under the realistic chronological split the empirical radial law has a **distribution-shift floor**: radial_W1(train-norms, test-norms) = **1.386** (markets grew more volatile: train norm q99=18.5 vs test 24.0). RAFM's radial (1.42) essentially reaches this floor but *cannot beat it* — so the "radial matched by construction" advantage is **bounded by radial stationarity** and here evaporates (RAFM ≈ Gaussian-FM on radial; source-only is worse). RAFM nonetheless **wins on every directional/global metric** (ks, sliced_w1, angular_sw, mmd; dir_sliced_w1 0.013 vs 0.016, cr_sliced_w1 0.130 vs 0.150) — the path still helps directions. RAFM is slightly worse on the q995 tail quantile (source underestimates the recent tail). This directly answers the reviewer concern that RAFM's metrics may be favored by radial matching: under realistic shift, the radial gain disappears while the directional gain persists.

### 5.13 E9 — Anisotropy scaling (new ablation, addresses "outside heavy-tailed regime")
Controlled **Gaussian** base (light-tailed, so anisotropy is the sole varying factor), d=32, mixing-matrix condition number k ∈ {1,3,10,30,100,300} (covariance condition number ≈ k²: 1, 9, 99, 890, 10⁴, 9·10⁴). Methods: gaussian_fm, source_only_empirical, rafm_empirical, 3 seeds. Files: `tables/E9_aniso.*`, `figures/E9_aniso.png`, `raw_results/E9_aniso/`. radial_w1 (mean):

| method | k1 | k3 | k10 | k30 | k100 | k300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gaussian_fm | 0.018 | 0.585 | 0.382 | 1.96 | 8.44 | 37.4 |
| source_only_empirical | 0.015 | 0.088 | 0.438 | 1.43 | 3.83 | 24.0 |
| rafm_empirical | **0.010** | **0.037** | **0.074** | **0.229** | **0.980** | **3.25** |

**Anisotropy alone drives RAFM's advantage — even with light tails.** As the covariance condition number grows, Gaussian-FM's radial error explodes (0.018→37.4) because the anisotropic Gaussian's radial law (‖A z‖) departs from the isotropic χ implied by the N(0,I) source; RAFM's eCDF source tracks it and stays far lower (0.010→3.25, ~11× better than Gaussian-FM at k=300). sliced_w1 shows the same. Once again **source-only (Euclidean) degrades like Gaussian-FM at high anisotropy** (24.0 at k=300) while the norm-preserving spherical path keeps RAFM bounded — so the path, not just the source, is essential. This gives a controlled, heavy-tail-free confirmation of the real-data Weather finding.

### 5.14 E-Weather-bigmodel — higher-capacity architecture (addresses R-architecture)

Controlled test on the Weather dataset (the most convincing new real dataset) with a **Residual MLP, width 256, 4 residual blocks** (≈8× the parameters), identical protocol, 3 seeds. Files: `tables/E_weather_bigmodel.*`. radial_w1 / sliced_w1 (mean), small MLP → big resMLP:

| method | radial (3×128 → resMLP256) | sliced (3×128 → resMLP256) |
|---|---|---|
| gaussian_fm | 0.179 → 0.311 (±0.21) | 0.085 → 0.105 |
| source_only_empirical | 0.380 → 0.185 | 0.094 → 0.092 |
| rafm_empirical | **0.087 → 0.087** | 0.075 → 0.081 |

**The trend persists with more capacity.** RAFM remains best on radial/KS/sliced. Its radial value is **exactly unchanged** (0.087) — expected, because with tangent projection the radial law is fixed by the source, *independent of the network* (so extra capacity cannot change it, by construction). Gaussian-FM does **not** improve with capacity (radial 0.18→0.31, high variance ±0.21 — signs of instability on the small N=3508 weather train set), refuting the "the gap is just an under-parameterised baseline" concern. Extra capacity mostly helps source-only's radial (0.38→0.19) but it still trails RAFM.

### 5.15 E-Finance split ablation — chronological vs random (addresses R-metric / radial-matching)
Diagnostic ablation isolating the train→test radial shift. **Chronological is the main result** (realistic, no leakage); random is the stationary control. Files: `tables/E_finance_randomsplit.*`, `diagnostics/finance_split_radial_shift.json`.

Irreducible radial floor W1(train-norms, test-norms): **chronological 1.386** vs **random 0.142** (random: train norm mean 6.59 ≈ test 6.60 — approximately stationary). radial_w1 of the generators:

| method | chronological | random |
|---|---|---|
| gaussian_fm | 1.42 | 0.252 |
| source_only_empirical | 1.71 | 0.351 |
| rafm_empirical | 1.42 (neutral) | **0.175 (best)** |

**This cleanly proves the neutral chronological result is a distribution-shift effect, not a learning failure.** Remove the shift (random split) and RAFM's radial drops to 0.175 — essentially the floor (0.142) — and RAFM becomes the best method again (0.175 vs Gaussian-FM 0.252 vs source-only 0.351), also best on ks (0.020) and sliced (0.057). So: RAFM *reaches the best achievable radial given the source*; under realistic non-stationarity that floor is high and the radial advantage disappears, but that is a property of the data (radial non-stationarity), not of RAFM failing to learn. Presented as an ablation; the chronological result stays primary.

NFE×solver sweeps on fixed checkpoints for **PIV d64** and **Weather** (RK4, projection on for RAFM). Files: `raw_results/E4_nfe_solver/{piv,weather}_{rafm,gaussian}.csv`. RAFM radial_w1 vs NFE:

| 4 | 0.209 | 0.606 |
| 8 | 0.038 | 0.108 |
| 20 | 0.041 | 0.113 |
| 100 | 0.041 | 0.113 |

RAFM **converges by ~NFE 8** (2 RK4 steps) on both real datasets — identical to the synthetic finding — with inference <0.05 s for 10⁴ samples at NFE 100. MSGM NFE curves are **not** included: they require a trained MSGM checkpoint and its bespoke Stratonovich sampler; MSGM training on these datasets is the compute bottleneck (see §5.17).

### 5.17 MSGM on the new real datasets (addresses R-MSGM) — status
MSGM is the reviewer-cited non-Gaussian baseline, so it must appear in the new real-data comparisons. Runner: `scripts/run_msgm_real.py` (reuses `baselines/msgm_runner.py`; same eCDF norm source, same seeds/metrics, matched budget 10k steps / batch 4096 (finance) / 2048 (weather), 3 seeds each). **Cost:** MSGM is O(d²–d³) per step — measured **~2.2 s/step at d=49 and d=96** ⇒ ~5.4 h per 10k-step seed, ~32 GPU-h total for the 6 runs on this single GPU (vs 35 s/run for RAFM). All 6 runs **completed at full budget** (3 seeds × 2 datasets).

Final results (3 seeds):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

But **RAFM wins the global/directional metrics (sliced_w1, MMD) on both** (finance 0.174 vs 0.182; weather 0.075 vs 0.120) and trains **~540–550× faster**. Honest, non-"wins-everywhere": MSGM is not dominated on radial; RAFM's edge is directional quality plus a ~540× training-speed advantage.

## 6. Negative / inconclusive results (explicit)
1. **Toy2D d=2:** RAFM angular/global quality clearly worse than all baselines (§5.5) — a genuine failure regime.
2. **Gaussian control absolute values** run lower than the paper (both gaussian_fm and RAFM) though the qualitative ordering holds — likely torch/seed/version differences; flagged, not resolved.
3. **On-disk `outputs/` ≠ paper batch size** (256 vs 4096) — the shipped artifacts are a lower-batch variant; matters for gaussian_fm.
4. **d16 angular_sw_mean** improvement from the path is within seed noise (path helps clearly only from d32 upward).
5. **Finance radial is NEUTRAL (not a win):** under the chronological split the eCDF source hits a distribution-shift floor (W1(train,test-norms)=1.386); RAFM's radial ≈ Gaussian-FM and *cannot* beat it. RAFM's advantage there is directional only. This is the clearest demonstration that the "radial matched by construction" metric does **not** automatically favor RAFM — it requires radial stationarity between train and test.
6. **Finance q995 tail quantile:** RAFM slightly worse than Gaussian-FM (0.38 vs 0.31) — the historical eCDF source under-estimates the recent tail.
7. **PIV directions:** the spherical path adds ~nothing over source-only on PIV directional metrics (cr_sliced_w1 tied) — PIV's benefit is essentially radial/source, unlike synthetic/finance/weather where the path also helps directions.
8. **Small-sample (E7):** at n≤1000 RAFM ≈ Gaussian-FM (underfits the spherical field); the advantage needs n≥~5000.
9. **Light-tailed low-d (E8 df=50, toy2d):** RAFM neutral or worse — its regime is heavy tails and/or higher dimension with anisotropy.
10. **Higher-capacity does not change RAFM's radial** (0.087→0.087) — arch-invariant by construction; and Gaussian-FM did not benefit from capacity (got noisier, 0.18→0.31±0.21) on the small weather train set. So the RAFM/Gaussian gap is not a small-MLP artifact.
11. **MSGM matches RAFM on radial on the new real datasets** (3 seeds, complete): finance tied at the shift floor; weather MSGM radial 0.099 within noise of RAFM 0.087, KS tied. So not every real-data comparison is a RAFM radial win — RAFM's real edge is on the directional metrics (sliced/MMD) and on training speed (~540×). MSGM is compute-bound (~5.4 h/seed at d=49/96 vs 35 s for RAFM).

## 7. Numerical diagnostics
- **Radius drift (spherical method):** projection ON → ~0 (1.7e-5 @ NFE100); OFF → ~1.28 regardless of NFE (E4). Euclidean methods change norm by design (not a defect).
- **Constraint:** slerp keeps ‖·‖=R analytically; sampler projects velocity tangentially each step; no explicit state re-projection.
- **Failures:** only the projection-OFF d=2 case NaNs (14.8%); all projected runs nan_rate 0 (d16/d32/gaussian/toy2d).
- **Preprocessing caveat:** PIV centering uses whole-dataset mean (pre-split) — mild statistic leakage inherited from MSGM; a train-only-centering robustness check is listed as future E10-extended (not run).

## 12. Concise overall summary (what the results actually show)
1. The paper's synthetic results **reproduce** at the documented batch size (4096); the on-disk artifacts used batch 256 and understate gaussian_fm.
2. RAFM's **radial** advantage is almost entirely the eCDF **source** correction (radial law is source-determined by construction — verified via ~0 drift).
3. The **spherical path** adds a real, norm-independent **angular/global** improvement (common-radial diagnostic; d32 attribution) that **grows with dimension** and is negligible/absent at d=2.
4. RAFM beats the strong non-Gaussian baseline **MSGM** on quality and is ~**80× faster**.
5. The previously **documented toy2d NaN limitation does not reproduce** with projection on; it is a projection-off d=2 artifact. But d=2 is a genuine **weak regime** for RAFM's angular quality.
6. **Tangent projection** (not NFE/step count) is what controls norm drift; RK4 needs only ~2–5 steps for convergence.
7. **Real-world coverage now spans three domains.** (a) **PIV** (turbulence): RAFM best on radial/KS, path adds little on directions. (b) **Finance** (heavy-tailed, chronological): radial advantage **evaporates** under train→test radial shift (bounded by a 1.386 floor) — RAFM neutral on radial but best on directions; the cleanest evidence that radial-matching does **not** automatically favor RAFM. (c) **Weather** (light-tailed, d=96, anisotropic): RAFM **best on all metrics** — showing the benefit is driven by **dimension + anisotropy**, not only heavy tails.
8. **Regime map (when RAFM helps / doesn't):** helps most with heavy tails and/or high dimension with anisotropy and sufficient data (n≥~5k) and radial stationarity; neutral on near-Gaussian low-d (E8 df=50) and under strong train/test radial shift (finance chronological radial); worse at d=2 and small n. RAFM never diverged (nan_rate 0 with projection across every dataset run).
9. **Robustness to reviewer concerns (round 2–3):** (a) *architecture* — the RAFM advantage persists with an 8× larger residual MLP on Weather (radial arch-invariant by construction; Gaussian-FM does not catch up). (b) *radial-matching favors RAFM?* — the finance chrono/random split ablation shows RAFM only reaches the *achievable* radial floor; under non-stationarity that floor is high and the radial win vanishes (neutral, not a failure). (c) *NFE/efficiency* — RAFM converges by ~NFE 8 on real PIV and Weather, inference <0.05 s. (d) *MSGM* — matched-budget 3-seed runs completed on both new real datasets: MSGM ties RAFM on radial/KS but RAFM wins the directional metrics and is ~540× faster.
